# table

A wavetable synth for the bass, lead, arp and chord tracks: 42 of our own band-limited waves on a 7 × 6 grid, and a
smooth 2-D morph through it. P1 moves along a bank, from its gentlest wave to its most intense; P2 moves between
the seven banks (analog, organ, vocal, resonance, hard sync, FM, metal). Both are interpolated sample by sample, so
turning a knob or pointing the LFO at P1 or P2 never steps or clicks. Every note reads band-limited copies of the
waves (one per octave), and above C6 the engine adds up the waves' first seven harmonics itself, so high notes stay
clean. The OP-Z's own track filter (the filter and resonance knobs)
shapes the tone after it.

| Control | Function |
|---|---|
| P1 **position** | moves through the six waves of the current bank, interpolated in between: the waves sit at **0, 51, 102, 153, 204 and 255** (wave = P1 × 5 / 255; P1 25 is half wave 0, half wave 1). In every bank the waves get brighter or more intense from 0 to 255 (the grid is below). Wave 0 of each bank is its gentlest, wave 5 its harshest |
| P2 **bank** | moves through the seven banks, interpolated in between: **analog 0, organ 43, vocal 85, reso 128, sync 170, fm 213, metal 255** (bank = P2 × 6 / 255). Between two banks you hear both at the same position, crossfaded. The banks are ordered so neighbours blend well: analog → organ (both plain harmonic spectra), organ → vocal (static formants), vocal → reso (one formant peak), reso → sync (a moving peak with an edge), sync → fm (bright, dense), fm → metal (sparse, bell-like). Together P1 and P2 are a bilinear morph over the whole grid: the four waves around the point, mixed |
| velocity | level (linear: 64 is −6 dB) **and position**: the note plays at P1 + (velocity − 100) / 2, so soft notes sit on gentler waves (velocity 127: 13.5 steps up, 60: 20 down, 1: 49.5 down; 100 is the knob as set). The offset is fixed per note; the clamp at 0 and 255 keeps it on the grid |
| attack, decay, sustain, release | the OP-Z envelope on the level. The engine steps it every 4 samples (the host's per-sample rule with coef⁴ and 4 × the step) and interpolates in between, which saves ~20 cycles per sample: exact in decay and release, within 0.3 % of the per-sample level during attacks (3 % within the 1 ms ramp of attack 0) |
| filter, resonance | not used: they are the OS's own track filter, which runs after the engine. Presets leave it neutral (128, 0) |
| pan, level | the track's pan and level (yellow and red dial, orange page), applied by the engine as TE's engines do (`opz_mix.h`): level is linear (128 = −6 dB, 0 = silent), pan a balance (centre: both sides full; hard left: right silent). The FX1/FX2 sends are taken after them. They follow the LFO (targets pan and volume) without zipping |

The grid (P2 down, P1 across):

| Bank (P2) | 0 | 51 | 102 | 153 | 204 | 255 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **analog** (0) | sine | triangle | saw | square | pulse 25 % | pulse 10 % |
| **organ** (43) | 8′ + 2⅔′ (flute and quint) | 8′ 4′ 2′ | 8′ 4′ 2⅔′ | 8′ 4′ 2⅔′ 2′ 1⅓′ | seven bars, the top ones softer | all seven bars full |
| **vocal** (85) | "oo" | "oh" | "ah" | "a" (cat) | "eh" | "ee" |
| **reso** (128) | saw through a resonant low-pass at 1.5 × the note (dark, round) | at harmonic 3 | 5 | 9 | 16 | 28 (thin, whistling) |
| **sync** (170) | hard-synced saw at 1.3 × the note | 1.9 × | 2.6 × | 3.5 × | 4.6 × | 6.4 × (the classic sync scream) |
| **fm** (213) | 1:1 FM, index 0.6 (soft, sine-like) | 1.2 | 2 | 3 | 4.4 | 6.2 (brassy, buzzy) |
| **metal** (255) | fundamental + harmonics 2 and 6 | + 11 | + 18 | + 27 | + 37 | + 50, brighter |

Organ bars are tonewheel drawbars on the note as 8′ (harmonics 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 = 8′, 4′, 2⅔′, 2′, 1⅗′, 1⅓′,
1′), 3 dB per bar step. Vowels are male formants (Peterson and Barney) laid out for A2: like every wavetable, they
move with the note (higher notes sound smaller). The metal partials are a free bar's (1, 2.76, 5.40, 8.93, 13.34,
18.64, 24.81) on a base of two harmonics, rounded to whole harmonics so the waves stay in tune and never beat: glassy
and bell-like rather than truly inharmonic. All waves have the same loudness (RMS); the few with a high crest factor
(narrow pulse, "oh"/"ah", the top of reso) are up to 3.7 dB quieter so they peak no more than 1.6 × the saw.

## How it works

The waves are generated by [`gen_tables.py`](gen_tables.py) (Python standard library only) from additive recipes:
shapes made of straight lines (saw, square, pulse, triangle, hard sync) get their exact Fourier coefficients, FM
comes from an FFT of the formula, organ, vocal, resonance and metal are written as spectra. Each wave is shifted so
its fundamental is in sine phase (so a morph between two waves never thins out half-way through cancellation of
their fundamentals), normalised to the same RMS, and written in **four mip levels**: 256, 128, 64 and 32 samples
holding harmonics up to 127, 63, 31 and 15, all at 15 bits, and as the **coefficients of its first seven harmonics**
(a_h cos + b_h sin, same scale) for the notes above the last mip. Each harmonic of a mip is raised by the inverse of
the read's loss at that point, so the response is flat on every level. `python3 engines/table/gen_tables.py` rewrites
[`table_waves.h`](table_waves.h); `--check` tells whether it is up to date, `--report` prints the levels and an
aliasing test of the mips.

Per block (64 samples) the engine:

1. picks the mip whose highest harmonic stays at or below **16 kHz** at the block's highest pitch: 127 harmonics up to
   B2, 63 for C3–B3, 31 for C4–B4, 15 for C5–C6, and above 1,067 Hz (C♯6 up) the **additive path**;
2. **blends the grid into a short line**: a block passes over at most ~50 samples of the mip, so only that stretch is
   blended from the four waves around the morph point, with the point ramping along the stretch from the last
   block's position to this one's: P1 and P2 move sample by sample, never in block steps;
3. reads the line once per sample with a 4-point Hermite (Catmull-Rom) interpolation, times the envelope;
4. when the mip changes (the pitch crossed an octave boundary, by a bend, a glide or vibrato), plays the old and the
   new mip (or the additive path) over the block and crossfades: no click, the top octave of harmonics fades in or
   out.

The **additive path** replaces the old 7-, 3- and 1-harmonic mips: per block it blends the four grid corners'
coefficients at the morph point, each harmonic times a band gain (full up to 16 kHz, fading out by 18.76 kHz, so
harmonics leave one by one as a note rises instead of an octave at a time); the block's start is the last block's end,
kept in the voice, and when the two differ (P1, P2 or the band moved) the coefficients ramp sample by sample. A
block where nothing moved blends nothing. Per sample: sine and cosine of the phase from a 7th-order polynomial
(within 2.3 LSB, smooth), the higher harmonics from the Chebyshev recurrence z(h+1) = 2 cos t · z(h) − z(h−1), and the
weighted sum, all in pairs of 16-bit lanes (sine and cosine side by side: one Blackfin vector instruction per step,
seven harmonics as one straight run).

The tables are 2 × oversampled, which puts the interpolation's first images between 16 and 32 kHz: what folds back
lands above 12 kHz, and the second images near 44.1 kHz are 42–84 dB down in the kernel; the additive path has no
images at all. In the simulator ([`tests/alias.c`](tests/alias.c): held notes, the strongest component that isn't a
harmonic of the note, below 11 kHz, relative to the strongest harmonic): saw −96 dB at A2, −73 dB at A3, −67 dB at
A4, −61 dB at A5 / C6; the brightest waves (pulse 10 %, sync 6.4, metal 5) −46 to −57 dB at A4, −43 to −51 dB at A5
(the 15-harmonic mip, C5–C6). From C♯6 up (the additive path): **−77.5 dB or lower on every wave tested up to F7**
(saw −90 to −97 dB; was −50 to −61 dB, and −40 to −46 dB on the brightest waves), and up to the 11 kHz top −78 dB or
lower except sync 6.4 at C8–F8 (−67 to −74 dB: only its weak first three harmonics are left in the band there, and
the arithmetic's floor is fixed).

Every preset: filter 128, resonance 0, pan 128, sends 0, no LFO (the LFO depth's byte encoding is not confirmed yet,
so the presets leave it as the other engines do; an LFO on P1 or P2 is the classic wavetable scan to try by hand).
Chord presets sit lower in level because several voices add up. Names fit the OP-Z's 11 characters.
`python3 tools/opz.py presets check engines/table` reports 20 presets, 0 errors, 0 warnings. Not yet tuned by ear
on the device.

and jumps the third difference of the output at block boundaries is never larger than inside the blocks: no steps.

## What to listen for on the device

- **The morph:** hold a note and turn P1 slowly, then fast, on each bank: smooth, no zipper, no clicks; then P2
  across the banks. Point the LFO (triangle, a slow free rate) at P1 or P2: a continuous scan.
- **High notes:** play the analog saw and the sync and metal banks from C5 up to C8, and bend up and down across an
  octave: the tone should stay clean (no whistling tones moving against the bend), the top harmonics just fading
  in and out at the octave boundaries.
- **Velocity:** step velocities on the pluck arp or wave bass: soft notes darker (an earlier wave), hard notes
  brighter.
- **Levels:** compare the analog saw (P1 102, P2 0) with macro's saw at the same note: about the same level.
  Sweep P1 and P2 under a held note: the loudness should stay even.
- **Low notes:** the wave bass and sub organ at octave −2 and −3: full, no grit; the sub organ nearly a sine.
- **CPU:** table on all four synth tracks with full chords, knobs or the LFO moving: listen for crackles and
  whether the OS kills releasing notes (the runbook's step 11).

Original code and waves, MIT licence. The 2-D morph follows the idea of Mutable Instruments' Plaits wavetable model
(an interpolated grid of waves) and the PPG Wave / OP-XY wavetable synths; no code or tables from any of them (no TE

(1952). Hermite interpolation: Catmull-Rom spline.
